# Bus Connector and Digital IO

This section of the schematic is responsible for handling the interconnection between the two CC lines on the USB-C ports, as well as all digital USB-PD communications.

## Cable orientation detection

Even though USB-C cables feature two CC lines, only one of them is used for communication at a time; the other is either left floating or used to power the cable's e-marker. 

Thus, when a cable is connected to Port 1 and the device is in Sink mode, it needs to figure out which CC line is actually usable for communication. In Observer mode, the device needs to figure out which CC line is being used _by either port,_ and then connect them accordingly so that the attached devices can communicate with each other.

To accomplish this dance, Dr. PD uses three small 2:1 multiplexers, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U503" />, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U504" />, and <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U505" />. Each of these multiplexers has two inputs and one output, and can be configured to connect either input to the output:

- <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U503" /> is capable of redirecting either CC line from Port 1 on to one of the inputs of <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U504" />.
- <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U505" /> is likewise capable of redirecting either CC line from Port 2 on to the other input of <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U504" />.
- <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U504" /> is then responsible for either connecting the two selected CC lines together or leaving them isolated from each other.

The cable orientation mechanism works in slightly different ways depending on whether the device is in Sink mode or Observer mode.

### Sink mode

In Sink mode, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U504" /> is always configured to isolate the two CC lines from each other, since only Port 1 is used for communication. This prevents a device or cable connected to Port 2 from interfering with the signaling between Port 1 and Dr. PD's own internal transmitter.

When Dr. PD enters Sink mode, the PD Role Resistors circuit first places the appropriate pull-down resistor on both CC lines of Port 1. The Analog Monitor then measures both lines. If a source is connected, its pull-up resistor and Dr. PD's pull-down resistor establish a characteristic voltage on the active CC line. The firmware currently recognizes an attached source when the measured CC voltage is greater than 0.75 V and less than 2.0 V, then debounces the detection before selecting that line for communication. The firmware then configures <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U503" /> to route that line to Dr. PD's receiver and transmitter. If the source is working correctly, it starts sending PD messages that can then be processed by the firmware.

### Observer mode

In Observer mode, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U504" /> is initially configured to isolate the two ports. The firmware activates a state machine that examines all four CC lines for one whose voltage exceeds the configured 0.75 V source-detection threshold. After debouncing the detection, it records that line and port as the source candidate, then cycles through the two CC lines of the other port, connecting them through <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U504" /> to the selected CC line of the source port in turn until it finds one that pulls down the line to a voltage consistent with the presence of a sink device's 5.1kΩ pull-down resistor. Once it finds a valid CC line on the sink port, it locks that as the port choice for the sink, and leaves <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U504" /> configured to connect the two selected CC lines together.

The process has several failure modes, which the firmware handles by backing the state machine off of its port assignments as needed. This may be necessary, for example, if one or both of the devices attached to the USB-C ports are dual-role devices, in which case they alternate their connectivity between source and sink mode, and several attach cycles may be required until both devices align in a compatible way.

:::info
Note that this is a non-standard process that is not part of the USB-PD specification, since the specification assumes that the two devices are connected directly to each other, and thus can communicate with each other directly. 

Dr. PD defeats this assumption by interposing itself between the two devices and requiring two cables, which may be of different lengths and orientations, and thus may not be able to communicate with each other directly.
:::

## CC edge detector

<ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U502" label="U502A" /> converts the analog CC waveform into the digital `CC_IN` signal by comparing it with a programmable reference voltage. Its output changes state on both upward and downward threshold crossings. The firmware treats the first low-to-high transition after an idle period as the start of a frame, then measures the time between successive falling and rising transitions to decode the BMC signal. The reference is created by feeding a PWM signal from the firmware into <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R508" />, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R509" />, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R510" />, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="C503" />, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="C504" />, and <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="C506" />, which, together with <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U501" label="U501A" />, form a second-order active low-pass filter.

By default, the reference voltage is set to 0.7 V, which has proven to work with the vast majority of the USB-C devices that we have tested. It can be adjusted through **Device → Calibrate → Internal settings…** in the frontend's BMC Decoder configuration or by using the corresponding SCPI command.

## CC transmission circuit

The last bit of functionality contained in this section of the schematic is used by the firmware when it needs to transmit data on the CC lines. <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R501" />, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R502" />, and <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R503" /> form a simple voltage divider that generates a reference voltage of about 1.1V. This, in turn, is buffered by <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="U501" label="U501B" />, providing a stiff voltage source that represents the “high” state of the CC line, as required by the USB-PD specification.

The transmitter uses separate `CC_OUT` and `CC_OUT_EN` signals. During transmission, `CC_OUT` carries the BMC waveform while `CC_OUT_EN` enables the analog output stage. Between messages, the firmware deasserts `CC_OUT_EN`, placing the transmitter in a high-impedance state so that it does not disturb the CC line's normal bias voltage.

`CC_OUT` controls two complementary current-mirror branches. When `CC_OUT` is low, the PNP pair <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q502" /> and <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q505" /> sources current from the buffered 1.1 V reference into the transmitter output. When `CC_OUT` is high, the NPN pair <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q504" /> and <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q503" /> sinks current from the output to ground. <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R14" />, <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R504" />, and <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R505" /> establish the reference currents. By charging and discharging the CC-line capacitance with controlled currents, the mirrors limit the waveform's slew rate, reducing high-frequency energy, ringing, and EMI while producing edges compatible with the USB-PD physical-layer requirements.

There is one more wrinkle to deal with: The CC lines are normally held at a voltage between approximately 0.9V and 1.5V by the combination of the source's pull-up resistor and the sink's pull-down resistor. Because the transmission current mirrors are fairly low impedance, this means that they would normally tend to pull down the line voltage below the normal range, which might inhibit communication on the line or even be interpreted as a disconnection event.

<ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q501" /> connects the current-mirror output to the selected CC line only while transmission is enabled. <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R506" /> pulls <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q501" />'s gate to ground by default, keeping it off and isolating the transmitter. When the firmware asserts `CC_OUT_EN`, the signal drives <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q501" />'s gate through <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="R16" />, turning it on and allowing the sourcing and sinking branches to drive the CC line. Deasserting `CC_OUT_EN` turns <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q501" /> off and returns the transmitter to its high-impedance state.

:::note
<ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q501" /> may appear backwards compared with a typical N-channel MOSFET switch, but its orientation is deliberate: when <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q501" /> is off, its body diode is oriented to prevent current from entering the output stage from the CC line. When <ComponentRef revision="R2605-A" reference="Q501" /> is on, its channel allows the transmitter to both source and sink current.
:::
