# Analog Monitor

The Analog Monitor section measures VBUS voltage and current, the CC-line voltages of both USB-C ports, system ground, the 1.65 V current-sense reference, the +3.3 V supply rail, and the separate 3.3 V ADC reference voltage.

## VBUS current monitoring

The shunt resistor R202 sits in series with the VBUS line at Port 1, producing a 5mV/A voltage drop proportional to the current flowing through it.

U303, an INA240A2 bidirectional current-sense amplifier, amplifies this voltage drop by a factor of 50V/V. At Dr. PD's maximum supported current of 6A, the 30mV voltage drop across R202 therefore corresponds to a 1.5V change at the output of U303. This is sufficient to cover the 0-5A range of the USB-PD specification, with some headroom to make characterizing the response of DUTs to overcurrent events.

Since the current can flow in both directions, U303 is biased with a 1.65 V reference voltage generated by U304, a TL432B adjustable shunt regulator. This allows the output of U303 to swing above and below the 1.65 V reference voltage. Across Dr. PD's supported current range of −6 A to +6 A, the designed output range is approximately 0.15 V to 3.15 V, suitable for the microcontroller's ADC to read and process.

We chose R202 to minimize the burden voltage on the VBUS line while still producing a reasonable voltage drop for U303 to amplify. To minimize noise and maximize the accuracy of the circuit, we use Kelvin connections of exactly the same length between the shunt resistor and the amplifier.

Despite this, there are still enough factors at play that reduce the absolute accuracy of the current measurement, including amplifier offset voltage, resistor and shunt reference tolerances, and PCB trace resistance. 

To account for these errors, factory calibration records the raw current reading at 0.5 A intervals from 0 A through 6 A. The firmware stores this calibration table in the device's non-volatile memory. During normal operation, it determines the calibrated current magnitude by linearly interpolating between the two surrounding table entries, then reapplies the measured current direction. During each VBUS acquisition, the firmware also samples system ground and the 1.65 V reference. It uses the ground sample as the ADC baseline and the measured 1.65 V reference as the zero point for the current measurement.

## VBUS voltage monitoring

Resistors R301 and R302 form a voltage divider that scales VBUS by a theoretical factor of 1/18.405, producing a 0-3.26V swing for the 0-60V range that Dr. PD supports. Since USB-PD maxes out at 48V when in EPR mode, this is more than sufficient to cover the entire range of voltages that can be applied to the VBUS line. The resulting sense voltage is buffered by U301A, and fed to the ADC of the microcontroller for processing.

Naturally, the voltage divider is not perfect, and the actual scaling factor will be different based on tolerances, voltage offsets, device on-time, temperature, and so on. Just like the current-sensing signal, VBUS voltage is factory-calibrated using a persisted correction table with one entry for each raw integer-voltage bucket from 0 V through 60 V. 

For readings of 1 V or greater, the firmware linearly interpolates between the two surrounding correction entries and adds the result to the raw scaled voltage. Readings below 1 V bypass the correction table because the calibration fixture cannot provide a reliable calibration point at 0 V due to the reference voltage to U303 bleeding through.

## Voltage monitoring multiplexer

Since they are critical measurements for which the highest accuracy is required, current and voltage sensing on VBUS both have separate circuitry that feeds directly into a dedicated ADC channel of the microcontroller.

Several other voltages in the system are not as critical, and so they are multiplexed using U302, a 74HC4051D analog switch. The microcontroller selects an input using the `CC_VSENSE_x` signals. U301B buffers the selected output from U302, after which R303 and R304 scale it by approximately 0.662 before it reaches the microcontroller's ADC. This allows the measurement path to accommodate inputs of up to approximately 5 V within the ADC's 3.3 V range.

In addition to the individual CC lines on both ports, the multiplexer monitors system ground, the 1.65 V current-sense reference, the +3.3 V supply rail, and the separate 3.3 V ADC reference voltage. The +3.3 V channel measures the system supply rail, while the ADC-reference channel measures the voltage used as the ADC's reference.
