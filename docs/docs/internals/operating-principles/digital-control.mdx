# Digital Control

This section of the schematic contains the digital control logic for the system. It is based on the RP2354, the same microcontroller used in the Raspberry Pi Pico 2 (but with built-in flash memory, thus obviating the need for an external flash chip).

With some minor differences, the circuitry here is essentially the same as that found in the Pico 2 reference design. We have replaced the Pico 2's buck converter with a linear regulator to reduce noise, since this is more important than power conversion efficiency in our design.

## Reset and debug circuits

The SW601 and SW602 switches can be used to force the RP2354 into DFU mode and reset it respectively. In the event of a firmware fault that “bricks” the device, you can push SW601, and then press and release SW602 to reset the RP2354 into DFU mode. You can then reflash the firmware using the Raspberry Pi Imager or other tools.

J601 exposes the RP2354's SWD interface for debugging and programming. You can use a Raspberry Pi Pico or other SWD programmer to connect to this header and flash firmware or debug the device.

## Status LED

LED601 can be used to debug the device. While it is not exposed on the front panel, it flashes a series of blinks to indicate the device's current status. The blink patterns are described in the [firmware's source code](https://github.com/T76-org/drpd/blob/main/firmware/lib/app/status_led.hpp).

## Voltage references

U605 is used to generate a reasonably accurate 3.3V reference voltage for the RP2354's ADC. The firmware's calibration routines are designed to compensate for the ±1.8% accuracy of this reference to provide accurate voltage and current readings.

## ADC channels

As discussed in the [Analog Monitor section](../analog-monitor), two separate ADC channels are dedicated to VBUS voltage and current sense measurements, while a third one is used for all other analog measurements. We did this because VBUS measurements are the most critical for the device's operation, and we wanted to ensure that they would have the most direct and accurate paths to the ADC. The other analog measurements are less critical, and can tolerate a bit more noise and inaccuracy.
