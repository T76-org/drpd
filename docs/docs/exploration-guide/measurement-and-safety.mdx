# Measurement and safety guardrails

Dr. PD makes USB-PD behavior easier to see, but high-power USB-C testing is
still live power work. Use known-good cables, an appropriate load, intentional
protection thresholds, and external instruments when the result matters.

## Electrical and measurement ranges

Use these limits when planning a setup or interpreting a capture.

| Item | Value | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VBUS voltage measurement | 0-60 V | Stay inside the instrument limit and the ratings of the source, cable, load, and fixture. |
| VBUS current measurement | ±6 A | Current direction depends on the test mode and power flow. Confirm sign convention before comparing results. |
| Display and command resolution | 10 mV / 10 mA | Resolution is not the same as absolute accuracy. |
| Preliminary factory-calibrated accuracy | Typical +/-1% voltage and +/-2% current, full-scale | These specifications are preliminary, typical, and subject to change. Cross-check when accuracy matters. |
| Sink requests | Up to 48 V / 5 A / 240 W | Requires compatible protocol state, source capability, cable capability, load, and EPR/PPS/AVS support where applicable. |
| OVP/OCP thresholds | Up to 60 V / 6 A | Thresholds are protection settings, not operating recommendations. |

## Reading captures carefully

Dr. PD correlates decoded USB-PD traffic with VBUS voltage and current readings.
That is useful for attach, contract changes, load steps, disconnects, hard
resets, and protection events.

It helps to keep three kinds of evidence separate:

- **Protocol evidence:** messages observed, decoded, filtered, exported, or
  timed in the Message Log.
- **Electrical readings:** VBUS voltage, current, power, charge, or energy
  reported during the same interval.
- **External confirmation:** what a DMM, oscilloscope, electronic load, or power
  analyzer measured at the same point in the setup.

For timing-sensitive or signal-integrity questions, use an oscilloscope or logic
analyzer with the relevant CC/VBUS nodes. A decoded message log is strong
protocol evidence, but it is not an analog signal-integrity measurement by
itself.

## Calibration caveats

Dr. PD ships factory-calibrated, and the current preliminary typical accuracy
targets are +/-1% voltage and +/-2% current full-scale. Those values are not a
blanket guarantee for every setup, environmental condition, or future hardware
revision.

Calibration affects all measurements and protections. If calibration data is
wrong, VBUS readings can be wrong and OVP/OCP decisions can be wrong. Before
changing calibration, read the [calibration guide](../how-to/calibrating.mdx)
and use stable reference equipment.

For absolute accuracy work, compare more than one operating point. A single 5 V
reading does not characterize the 0-60 V voltage range, and a single load point
does not characterize the current range.

## OVP/OCP behavior

Dr. PD provides configurable overvoltage protection (OVP) and overcurrent
protection (OCP). These protections disconnect power delivery rather than
regulate it:

- OVP/OCP are cut-off protections, not voltage clamps or current limiters.
- Thresholds can be configured up to 60 V for OVP and 6 A for OCP.
- A triggered OVP or OCP condition latches and requires a manual reset before
  normal operation resumes.
- Threshold settings are stored on the device, so check the active limits before
  changing the setup.
- The protections depend on the same measurement calibration used for displayed
  VBUS readings.

Choose thresholds for the specific source, cable, load, and expected contract.
If a protection trip occurs, check the requested contract, cable, load behavior,
Dr. PD calibration, and independent measurements before drawing conclusions.

## EPR and high-power cautions

Sink mode can request high-power USB-PD contracts up to 48 V / 5 A / 240 W when
the source, cable, protocol negotiation, and load all support that condition.
That is enough power to damage equipment or start a fire if the bench setup is
wrong.

Before attempting EPR, AVS, PPS, or other high-power tests:

- Use a cable rated for the requested current and mode.
- Confirm the source actually advertises the requested contract.
- Use an external load rated for the requested voltage, current, and power.
- Set OVP/OCP thresholds intentionally rather than relying on defaults.
- Keep the test attended and stop if cables, connectors, the source, the load,
  or Dr. PD heat unexpectedly.

If a high-power request fails, the limiting factor may be the cable, contract
selection, EPR entry state, load, protection settings, or thermal behavior, not
just the source.

## Port 2 lockout and reverse polarity

When Dr. PD operates as a sink on Port 1, Port 2 is disconnected from the USB-PD
bus and VBUS line. This lockout protects a device that might have been left on
Port 2, but a simple setup with unused devices removed is easier to understand.

The banana connectors include reverse-polarity protection for cases where an
external source or load is connected backwards. If it trips, Dr. PD disconnects
the banana connector VBUS path; the control app does not present this as an
OVP/OCP event. Treat an unexpected 0 V reading at the banana connectors as a
reason to stop and inspect wiring before continuing.

## External cross-checks

Useful cross-checks include:

- Comparing Dr. PD voltage readings with a DMM at the banana connectors.
- Comparing Dr. PD current readings with the electronic load readback or a
  current probe/shunt suitable for the expected current.
- Capturing VBUS and CC with an oscilloscope while Dr. PD records decoded
  protocol traffic.
- Recording cable identity, source model, selected contract, load setting,
  protection thresholds, firmware/software version, and calibration state.

Dr. PD can make USB-PD behavior visible and repeatable. The bench setup,
external validation, and safety decisions still matter.
